About the Lab

Ideas. Built. Tested. Iterated.

VaporWare Labs is a growing collection of engineering projects, experiments, and shared knowledge.

Projects range from autonomous vehicles and smart-home systems to project-management software, local artificial intelligence, self-hosted infrastructure, and documented cooking experiments.

The lab is not limited to one technical field or product. It is a place to follow curiosity, build useful things, preserve what was learned, and make the results available to others.

Financial Software

vaporFUND

A Django-based portfolio allocation assistant designed to maintain a target investment allocation primarily through new contributions rather than forced rebalancing.

  • Multiple investment accounts
  • Account-specific target allocations
  • Contribution-driven purchase recommendations
  • Whole-share purchase planning
  • Multi-purchase recommendation workflows
  • Account-aware holdings and contribution history

  • Automated test coverage

Current focus: expanding multi-account workflows, refining the dashboard and account-settings experience, and supporting real portfolio accounts with known cost basis.

Robotics / Embedded Systems

vaporEYES

A 3D-printed robotic eye platform combining embedded control, mechanical design, electromechanical integration, and autonomous behavior.

  • ESP32-C3 control
  • Independent pan and tilt servos
  • External 5 V servo power system
  • Calibrated 50°–130° safe motion envelope
  • Smooth spline-based eye movement
  • Autonomous behavior state machine
  • Idle, scan, fixation, and glance behaviors
  • 3D-printed mechanical structure

Current focus: designing the power-supply substructure and clean mounting for the ESP32 and WAGO terminals, followed by camera-based person and face tracking.

Planned vision architecture: Raspberry Pi with Camera Module 3 Wide performing person/face detection and sending target coordinates to the ESP32.

Design Philosophy

Build small. Test honestly. Preserve what matters.

Documentation is treated as a first-class engineering artifact. Whenever practical, projects preserve not only what was built, but why important decisions were made.

  1. 01Start with a useful experiment.
  2. 02Build the smallest workable version.
  3. 03Test it in the real world.
  4. 04Study the results.
  5. 05Improve the design.
  6. 06Document what was learned.
  7. 07Share the results.
